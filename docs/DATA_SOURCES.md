# Data sources and provenance

Every dataset in CONTINUUM is either **real and public** or **notional and labeled**. Nothing
comes from an employer, a program office or any non-public system. This page lists where each
item came from, how it was checked, and why it matters to the decision. (D-02, D-13, D-20, D-22)

## 1. Real, public inputs

| Item | Source | Publisher, date | Used for | How it was checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| End-of-life notice **CAAN-02OLLE763** | [PDF (Digi-Key mirror)](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/7131/CAAN-02OLLE763.pdf); original on the [Microchip PCN portal](https://www.microchip.com/en-us/support/product-change-notification) | Microchip Technology, 2025-06-06 | The demo trigger: 110 ProASIC3 PQFP-208 part numbers, LTB 2025-12-01, LTS 2026-12-01, the alternate-site cancellation caveat | Answer key verified against the notice text both ways: 110/110 (D-20) |
| Discontinuance notice **PDN2401** | [PDF (Digi-Key mirror)](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/5787/PDN2401_Rev1.0.0.pdf) | Intel (Altera FPGAs), 2024-01-15 | Second, longer notice for extraction: 120 leaded Cyclone / MAX part numbers with lead-free replacements (finish change → tin-whisker review) | 120/120 parts and 100/100 replacements, both ways (D-20) |
| Affected-part answer key | `data/reference/CAAN-02OLLE763_affected_parts.csv`, `data/reference/PDN2401_affected_parts.csv` | Transcribed from the two notices | Ground truth for scoring AIP extraction (230 part numbers) | `scripts/verify_ground_truth.py` (tested) |
| Notice metadata | `data/notices/notices.csv` | From the notices above | Dates, counts, source links | Dates read off the notices (D-20) |
| Service-life figures on the problem slide ("25 to 30 years" vs "4- to 7-year support cycle") | [Defense Standardization Program Journal, Jul/Sep 2014, "DMSMS Management: The Key to Higher Availability and Lower Costs", p. 4](https://www.dsp.dla.mil/Portals/26/Documents/Publications/Journal/140901-DSPJ.pdf) | Defense Standardization Program Office (DLA) | Why obsolescence is recurring work | Quoted directly |
| DMSMS process (notice → impact → resolution options) | [SD-22 DMSMS Guidebook](https://www.dau.edu/tools/t/SD-22-Diminishing-Manufacturing-Sources-and-Material-Shortages-(DMSMS)-Guidebook) | Defense Standardization Program Office | Steve's workflow steps and the courses of action (life-of-type buy, bridge buy, redesign) | Terminology and steps follow the guidebook |

The PDFs are linked, not committed (they are manufacturer documents). Inside Foundry the
notice text is loaded as `raw/raw_notice_text` (text layer only).

**Why these two notices.** ProASIC3 flash FPGAs are widely used in aerospace and defense
electronics, and CAAN-02OLLE763 contains two things a real engineer must not miss: a possible
cancellation of the notice, and a "no longer offered after" date that is the ship date, not
the buy date. PDN2401 is long and table-heavy, and its "replacement" changes the lead finish,
which defense programs review for tin whiskers. Together they test reading, not just parsing.

## 2. Notional inputs (fictional, labeled as such)

Program sustainment data (bills of materials, stock, demand) is not public. Everything below is
generated by `src/continuum/generate.py` with a fixed seed, then exported "messy" by
`src/continuum/raw.py` the way a customer spreadsheet would arrive.

| Item | Values | Basis | Why it is shaped this way |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Programs Heron, Kite, Petrel** | Airlift fleet (52 aircraft), jet trainer (96), surveillance radar upgrade (30) | Invented. Bird names were chosen so no name maps to a real U.S. program; any overlap with a real product name (for example, "Heron" is also a foreign UAV) is coincidental and unrelated | Three platform types show that one notice hits programs differently |
| **Steve** | DMSMS engineer | Invented persona | The operator whose decision the system supports |
| Assemblies (`HER-MCP-210` …) | Mission computer card, display unit, radar processor, etc. | Generic avionics nomenclature (CCA = circuit card assembly, LRU = line-replaceable unit) | Realistic places for an FPGA to sit |
| BOM part numbers | 4 real part numbers that are on the notices (A3P1000-1PQG208I, A3P250-PQG208I, M1A3P400-1PQG208I, EP4CE10E22I7), 1 real part number that is not (A3P1000-1FGG484I, a different package), all others prefixed `NTL-` | Real part numbers appear with their real status only; `NTL-` parts are obviously fictional so no real part's status is misrepresented | Exercises matching against real formats |
| Messy entries | `"  a3p250-pqg208i "`, `M1A3P400-1PQG208I-ND` (distributor suffix), `A3P1000-1FGG484I` (same die, different package) | Deliberate | Tests normalization, suffix handling, and the family-only trap |
| Stock on hand | Heron A3P1000: 312 units; others from a stated coverage | Assumption | Sets up the demo gap (13.8 months of stock vs a 24-month redesign) |
| Unit costs | e.g. $185 for the A3P1000 PQ208 | Order-of-magnitude assumption, not a quote | Makes the options comparable in dollars |
| Redesign lead time and cost | Heron 24 months, $1.45M | Assumption, editable per program | Drives the supply gap and the redesign option |
| Demand history | 72 months, intermittent (negative-binomial batches), slight upward trend | Simulated, seed 7 | Lumpy spares demand is why the forecast is a range |
| Holding cost, stress growth | 5%/yr each | Stated assumptions (D-08, D-10) | Makes the life-of-type buy pay for storage and shows how options behave if demand grows |
| Scenario date | 2025-06-10 | Four days after the real notice | Replays the real timeline so every date stays real (D-03) |

## 3. What this means for the results
- **Extraction scores are real**: real documents, a verified answer key.
- **Matching is real logic on notional BOMs**: the part numbers and traps are real formats.
- **Forecast and option numbers test the method, not reality**: they come from simulated demand
  and assumed costs. A pilot would replace section 2 with a program's own data and re-run every
  evaluation (deck, closing slide).
